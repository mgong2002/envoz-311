Product basics

What is Envoz 311?

Envoz 311 is an AI-native 311 platform that answers resident calls 24/7, understands intent in 70+ languages, resolves location against city GIS, and writes correctly classified cases into your existing CRM. It is a voice-to-outcome routing brain, not a chatbot: every case carries a confidence score, a rationale, and proof that the case number came from the system of record. Residents get answered before the second ring, staff get clean structured cases instead of voicemail chaos, and city leaders get measurable evidence of service improvement. See the Platform page for the full architecture, or watch the 90-second pothole demo.

Is Envoz a CRM replacement?

No. Envoz is overlay-first, built to arm the CRM you already own rather than replace it. Envoz writes into SeeClickFix today, with Accela and Salesforce via partner API, and is built on an Open311-style contract designed to extend to Tyler, QAlert, Cityworks, and OpenGov EAM. Residents and staff keep the tools and workflows they already know, and your system of record stays the system of record. A standalone mode exists for cities without a usable CRM, but the default posture is no rip-and-replace. Explore the integration matrix.

What dashboards do city managers get?

City managers get dashboards covering access, equity, speed, and cost — the four numbers councils actually ask about. That includes after-hours coverage, routing accuracy, SLA compliance, containment, and cost per interaction, alongside AI-discovered service gaps ranked by expected impact. The same console holds the learning ledger and the Nico Success Agent recommendation queue, so improvement work is visible in the same place as performance. One click generates a council-ready proof report from live evidence. Open the City Manager Success Dashboard to explore it with demo data.

How does Envoz support multilingual residents?

Envoz is 70+ language-ready, and every multilingual interaction is confidence-marked so staff can see language quality on the case itself. Residents report in their own language and get confirmations and SMS status updates in that language — in the demo, a Spanish streetlight report near a school zone becomes case VR-4330 with a visible 94% language-confidence mark. When language confidence dips, Nico asks a clarifying question or warm-transfers to a person instead of guessing at an address. See Resident Services for the full multilingual journey.

How quickly can a city configure Envoz?

About 12 minutes of conversation with Nico produces a configured demo city; a working sandbox tenant is ready in roughly 48 hours; and a production MVP typically lands in about four weeks. Configuration is conversational — departments, taxonomy, GIS layers, and schedules are set up by talking to Nico, not by filling out spreadsheets. The four-week production timeline is usually governed by city data cleanup (neighborhood names, schedule data, asset layers) rather than by the software. Integrations & deployment

How does Envoz integrate with SeeClickFix?

Envoz writes cases directly into SeeClickFix through its write API, and SeeClickFix remains the system of record. Envoz answers the call, classifies the request against your taxonomy, and resolves the location; SeeClickFix then issues the real case number, which Nico reads back to the resident. Write-backs retry with idempotency, fall back to structured email if the connector is degraded, and every write is audited. Residents and staff keep the SeeClickFix tools they know — Envoz closes the voice-channel loop on top. See the Integrations page for the full connector matrix.

Can Envoz work without an existing CRM?

Yes — Envoz standalone mode is built for cities that don’t have a usable CRM today. It provides a lightweight staff triage console, structured-email dispatch to departments, and thin case surfaces with local VR case numbers, so residents still get a real trackable case. Cities that later adopt a CRM can switch to overlay mode without re-onboarding residents or retraining staff. The Platform page explains when to choose each mode — and the decision guide below walks through the criteria.

What data does Envoz collect?

Envoz collects what a 311 case needs — the request, its location, and optional contact details for status updates — with consent records logged for every interaction. All data is tenant-scoped to a single city and never mixed across tenants, and the city owns its data outright: transcripts, cases, and learning evidence included. Learned improvements stay scoped to your city unless you explicitly approve sharing them as global evidence. The data model section of the Trust page covers retention, consent, and export in detail.

How does the platform support council reporting?

Envoz generates council-ready proof reports directly from live evidence — access gains, routing accuracy, equity coverage, SLA compliance, and cost avoided. A city manager can produce a 90-day modernization report in one click, with every headline metric traceable back to the underlying cases and the evidence log. In the demo world, Vista Robles shows 93.4% SLA compliance and 327 after-hours calls captured — all marked as illustrative demo data. Trust & safety

How does the voice agent prevent hallucinated case numbers?

Nico can only read back a case number that was actually issued by the system of record — case numbers are never generated by the AI. The platform waits for write-back proof, like “Case number issued by SeeClickFix at 9:44 PM”, before confirming anything to the resident, and that proof strip is visible on every case. If the write fails, Envoz retries with idempotency or falls back to a structured email — and tells the resident the truth about what happened. The same rule applies to repair dates: if Envoz doesn’t know, it says so. The Trust page documents every guardrail.

How does Envoz handle emergencies?

Emergencies are redirected to 911 immediately, and no ordinary 311 case is created. Every call starts with a safety gate — “Is anyone hurt or in immediate danger?” — before any classification or routing happens. Emergency handling is the one path where the platform never attempts to automate an outcome; it gets the caller to emergency services and steps out of the way. You can see the safety gate fire first in every voice journey.

What happens when confidence is low?

Low confidence triggers a warm transfer: Nico hands the caller to a person with full context attached — transcript, candidate classifications, and resolved location — so residents never repeat themselves. Non-urgent low-confidence reports are held in a human review queue instead of being routed on a guess: in the demo, a beehive in a bus shelter (41% confidence) waits in the learning queue with three candidate departments rather than becoming a misrouted case. Confidence thresholds are configured per city, and every below-threshold event becomes learning evidence. See it handled in Staff Triage.

What does “Powered By MetaEngage.ai” mean?

It means Envoz 311 runs on MetaEngage.ai’s civic AI infrastructure — the orchestration layer, Proxy Gate, audit system, and governed automation behind the product. MetaEngage.ai provides the engine; Envoz 311 is the 311-specific product built on top of it. The Proxy Gate is the control point where every AI action against a city system of record is checked and logged, which is how guarantees like “case numbers only from the CRM” are enforced in infrastructure rather than by policy alone. You’ll see the mark as a subtle watermark across the platform — read how the layers fit together on the Trust page.

How does Envoz keep humans in control?

Envoz never changes its own behavior without human approval — staff approve every rule, taxonomy change, and deployment, with explicit scopes from a single case up to a city-wide rule. Confidence and rationale are visible on every routing decision, low confidence means a warm transfer to a person, and every action is written to the audit trail. Approved changes are sandbox-tested against historical cases and versioned before they touch production. The operating principle across the platform: Envoz recommends, city staff approve. See governed autonomy on the Trust page.

Improvement & reporting

Can staff correct the AI?

Yes — correcting the AI is a first-class action in the staff triage console, and every correction becomes learning evidence. Staff choose the scope of what the system learns from each correction: this case only, a city-only rule, department memory, or global evidence — and nothing learned deploys without approval. In the demo, 19 water-pooling cases corrected from Streets to Utilities became a proposed storm-drain proximity rule that staff can sandbox-test before approving. Try the correction flow in Staff Triage.

How are service gaps discovered?

Service gaps are discovered from evidence patterns in the Civic Evidence Log: repeated unknown phrases, clusters of staff corrections, duplicate report clusters, SLA disparities between neighborhoods, and language-confidence dips. In the demo, 14 “storm grate burbling” reports that fell below routing threshold surfaced a missing taxonomy category, and Spanish calls near school zones revealed an address-confidence gap. Each surfaced gap ships with its evidence, a suggested fix, expected impact, and a departmental owner — staff decide what to act on. See the AI-discovered gaps for Vista Robles.

What is the Continuous Civic Improvement Loop?

The Continuous Civic Improvement Loop is the nine-step cycle — Listen, Understand, Route, Resolve, Measure, Learn, Recommend, Approve, Improve — that turns every 311 interaction into evidence and every pattern into an approvable improvement. Interactions are measured in the Civic Evidence Log, patterns become recommendations, and city staff approve every change before it is sandbox-tested, versioned, audited, and deployed. It is why an Envoz 311 program gets measurably better every week instead of just launching once.

What is the Nico Success Agent?

The Nico Success Agent is the AI success agent assigned to your city, monitoring your 311 program 24/7 for routing accuracy, equity, surge patterns, and connector health. It drafts safe, evidence-linked improvement recommendations — new routing rules, vocabulary additions, taxonomy candidates, data-quality fixes — and queues them for staff approval with sample cases and sandbox results attached. It never deploys a change on its own. In the demo, Vista Robles has 6 open recommendations with 3 ready for approval, projected to save 42 staff hours per month. Open the recommendation queue.
